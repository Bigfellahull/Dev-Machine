# Global Instructions

## Code Style

- Keep inline comments minimal and reserved for genuinely complex code. Only explain the **why**, never the **what** —
  the code itself should be readable enough. If the logic is straightforward, no comment is needed.
- Add language specific comments to all public methods and types.

## Writing

Apply these rules to all user-facing prose, including documentation, emails, chat messages, tickets, release notes, reports, policies, explanations, and other written content. Follow any task-specific writing instructions where they are more specific.

* Write in clear, natural language. The result should sound like it was written and edited by a competent human, not generated from a template.
* Preserve the original meaning, technical detail, terminology, defined terms, UI labels, requirements, and important qualifications.
* Use plain, direct language and relatively short sentences.
* Prefer active voice when it makes the sentence clearer or makes responsibility explicit.
* Remove repetition, filler, unnecessary adjectives, throat-clearing, and wordy explanations.
* Do not explain the obvious. Include background or justification only when it helps the reader understand the subject, decision, or requirement.
* Avoid overly formal, academic, bureaucratic, legalistic, marketing, or AI-sounding language unless the context genuinely requires it.
* Avoid canned transitions and stock phrases such as "furthermore", "it is important to note", "in order to", "leverage", "utilise", "seamlessly", "robust", and "powerful".
* Do not replace precise technical, security, legal, or domain terminology with vague everyday language.
* Prefer concrete statements over abstract claims. Say what changed, what something does, or what the reader needs to do.
* Do not manufacture benefits, motivations, examples, enthusiasm, or certainty that are not supported by the source material.
* Keep the tone appropriate to the context. Be professional and natural without becoming stiff, chatty, promotional, or artificially friendly.
* Vary sentence structure and paragraph length naturally. Do not make every section follow the same pattern.
* Use transitions only where they help the reader follow the text. Do not add connecting phrases simply to make prose sound polished.
* Do not deliberately add mistakes, slang, fragments, or other "humanising" imperfections.
* Preserve meaningful distinctions such as "must", "should", and "may". Do not strengthen or weaken requirements while rewriting.
* Prefer concise wording, but do not remove detail needed for accuracy, safety, compliance, or understanding.
* Use British English unless the task, source material, product terminology, or target audience requires another form of English.

Before returning substantial prose, make a final pass and remove wording that is repetitive, inflated, vague, unnecessarily formal, promotional, or recognisably AI-generated.

### Pasteable Output

When asked for text intended to be pasted directly into another system, such as an email, chat message, ticket, document, form, or similar destination:

* Return the pasteable content in plain text unless the user explicitly asks for Markdown or another format.
* Do not wrap lines mid-sentence. Each paragraph, sentence-only line, or bullet should remain unwrapped so copying does not introduce spurious line breaks.
* Do not surround the content with commentary, explanation, Markdown fences, quotation marks, or labels unless requested.
* Preserve intentional blank lines between paragraphs and sections.
